# Systems Designer — Round 1

Scope: core loop, progression structure, pacing, difficulty curve, economy math for Metr0niks. Source: `gdd.md` (Oct 20, 2026, with Oct 22 prototype additions).

## F1 — Gauntlet lets you keep scrap with no risk, which breaks Excursion's risk/reward loop
**Severity: BLOCKING**

**Source:** *Run rules*: "**Gauntlet:** the same waves with no penalty for losing. You keep your scrap and take no damage that needs repair. Gauntlet doesn't progress your mech." *Garage and drop pods*: "Gauntlet has no such loss." *Run rules*: "**Progression:** mechs progress only in excursions."

**Problem:** The whole progression model runs on scrap, and the garage turns scrap into parts, upgrades and repairs. If Gauntlet scrap is kept and can be spent in the garage, Gauntlet is a no-risk farm: same waves, no durability damage, no loss on death. That makes it strictly better than Excursion, and the extract-or-lose-everything tension in the core loop goes away. If Gauntlet scrap can't be spent, "you keep your scrap" means nothing, and Gauntlet gives no reward at all. The document says both "keeps scrap" and "doesn't progress your mech" without saying which one it means. The core loop isn't defined until this is settled. Possible fixes: Gauntlet scrap is cosmetic or not banked, it goes into a separate pool, or it pays out at a heavy discount.

## F2 — The prototype's "campaign levels" and zones don't fit the 5-wave excursion structure
**Severity: MAJOR**

**Source:** *Run rules*: "**Waves:** each excursion is 5 waves. Bosses come on waves 3 and 5. This is the only place the wave structure is defined." *Zones (prototype, 22 Oct)*: "a campaign opens them one level at a time … the Underpass (level 1), the Freight Yard (opens on level 2) and Smelter Row (opens on level 3); from level 4 the whole sector is one battlefield … the camera returns before wave 1 begins. Both players watch the same shot."

**Problem:** The Zones section adds a second progression axis, campaign levels, that appears nowhere in Run rules, Game modes or Economy. Several things are left unanswered. Is a "level" an excursion? Does "wave 1 begins" mean each level restarts the 5-wave count? Is level progress saved, and is it lost on death the way scrap is? Do zones gate scrap hotspots (the highway transports) and therefore income? "Both mechs land" and "both players" also clash with the single-player scope ("1 vs AI"). Pacing over a full playthrough, meaning how the arena widens and the difficulty rises, now depends on a system the canonical rules section says doesn't exist.

## F3 — All-or-nothing scrap plus durability decay sets up a death spiral with no catch-up
**Severity: MAJOR**

**Source:** *Run rules*: "**Extract:** clearing wave 5 extracts you and banks your scrap. Leaving early counts as a loss." "**On death:** you lose all scrap from the match." "**Durability:** at the end of an excursion, a share of the damage taken (TBD%) becomes durability damage. It lowers max health, heat tolerance and other stats until you repair it in the garage." *Economy*: "Repair durability damage | TBD | Yes".

**Problem:** A failed run costs the player twice. They bank no scrap, and if durability applies on a death (the text only says "at the end of an excursion", which is ambiguous), their mech is also weaker for the next run, with no scrap to repair it. The weaker mech is more likely to die again. The only floor is "Starter mechs … free and can't be lost," which means falling back to the bottom of the power curve and doesn't help a damaged owned build recover. A 5-wave run has two bosses (waves 3 and 5) and no early-extract option, so the cost of failure grows the deeper you get. The document doesn't define a partial payout, a checkpoint after the wave-3 boss, or a repair floor. Durability %, repair cost and scrap income are all TBD, so nothing shows the loop recovers from failure.

## F4 — Part tradeoffs bypass the five-stat model they're supposed to feed
**Severity: MAJOR**

**Source:** *Overview* pillar: "Each part changes weight, heat or power, so building your mech changes how it moves and fights." *Mech building*: "Every part adds to five stats … Base values and formulas for each stat are TBD." *Parts list*: Ablative slag-plating "30% slower"; Bunker-buster "torso turns 25% slower"; Reactor "Sets speed and thrust time in the air"; Jump-jets "Each jump adds 40% heat". *Damage regions*: "A disabled weapon stops firing and its weight is removed, so the mech gets faster … Speed always follows weight."

**Problem:** The garage promises "every change shows its effect on the five stats," but most tradeoffs in the starting pool are flat percentage modifiers that skip Power/Armor/Speed/Weight/Heat. Slag-plating is "30% slower" rather than adding Weight, and the Reactor sets speed and air time while Speed is supposed to come from the Speed stat minus Weight. Only the Oct 22 prototype parts use stat deltas ("+14 speed", "weight -12", "Power +20"). As written, the stat model and the parts table run side by side instead of linking, so the pillar "every part is a tradeoff" can't be checked or tuned. The weight-removal rule also rewards losing an arm with more speed, and there's no stated counterweight. That works against the build-matters pillar unless it's tuned on purpose.

## F5 — Scrap sinks overlap, every value is TBD, and rarity is defined two different ways
**Severity: MAJOR**

**Source:** *Economy*: "Scrap is the only currency. All values are TBD." Sinks: "Buy a part (higher rarity costs more) | TBD per tier | No", "Stat upgrade | TBD per level | Yes", "Higher-tier weapon | TBD per tier | Yes". "Repairs and upgrades keep scrap useful once every part is owned." *Parts list*: "parts come in five tiers: white, green, blue, purple and orange" versus the prototype "rarity uses three tiers for now (common, rare, epic)" with prices 15–140.

**Problem:** Three sinks seem to buy the same thing: a part's rarity tier, a "higher-tier weapon", and a "stat upgrade". The document doesn't say whether a higher-tier weapon is a separate purchase from a higher-rarity part, or whether stat upgrades stack on a part or on the frame. That leaves no clear power-progression axis to pace against. No scrap source has a value, so nobody can estimate runs-to-next-part, or check whether the prototype's 140-scrap Cold-fusion cell takes 2 runs or 20 to earn. The long-term sink claim ("once every part is owned") rests on repeatable sinks whose costs and caps are undefined. A five-tier design and a three-tier prototype exist side by side with no mapping between them. Before any tuning, the document needs one rarity scheme, a distinct purpose for each sink, and at least target income per run.

## Round 2 — Cross-examination

I read all five colleague reviews and re-checked their quotes against `gdd.md`. Most of the board agrees that the Gauntlet, zones and death-spiral problems exist. The disagreements are about which way to fix them, and several of the proposed fixes would break the economy if they were adopted on their own. The main thing I'm adding this round: my F1 (Gauntlet farm) and F3 (death spiral) are two sides of one lever and should be fixed together.

### Player Psychologist

**CONFLICT — player-psychologist F4 vs my F1 (Gauntlet payout).** The psychologist wants Gauntlet to pay "a small scrap payout" so practice doesn't feel wasted. adversarial-qa F1 and my F1 say any bankable Gauntlet scrap risks turning it into a no-risk farm. Both positions can hold, but only with numbers. Let `S` be the scrap from a clean 5-wave run, `p` the player's extract rate and `R` the expected durability repair cost. A rational player prefers Excursion only while `p·S − R > g·S`, where `g` is the Gauntlet payout rate. The players with the lowest `p` are the struggling players the psychologist is trying to protect, so for them a "small" payout soon becomes the better deal. That is fine as a deliberate catch-up valve. It is a problem when it happens by accident. My position: Gauntlet can pay, but `g` has to be set below the `p·S − R` of a median player and capped per day or per session. Better still, Gauntlet scrap should be **repair-only**, held in a separate pool that can only buy durability repairs (see the connection below). The psychologist's other suggestions (mastery goals, try-before-you-buy) don't touch the economy, and I support them without reservation.

**CONNECTION — player-psychologist F1 + F4 + my F1 + my F3.** Put these findings together and they describe one missing system. The death spiral needs a recovery source that doesn't cost scrap, and Gauntlet is a reward source with no defined purpose. A repair-only Gauntlet payout fixes both. A broke player with a degraded mech plays Gauntlet at no risk, earns enough to repair, and goes back into Excursion. Gauntlet can't fund parts, upgrades or tiers, so "Gauntlet doesn't progress your mech" stays literally true and the farm exploit is gone. adversarial-qa F4 also says "the only escape is Gauntlet scrap (see F1), which is meant not to progress you". A repair-only pool turns that contradiction into a rule the document can state.

**CONFLICT (partial) — player-psychologist F1's wave-3 partial bank.** I agree it's needed. The size of the bank still matters. With bosses on waves 3 and 5, a full bank at wave 3 would make waves 4–5 a pure bonus round, and the stakes ladder (narrative-critic F4) would flatten even further. I'd bank a fraction of wave 1–3 scrap at the wave-3 kill (for example 30–50%) and keep the rest at risk until extract. That keeps a reason to push on while taking away the "lose everything on wave 5" uninstall moment.

**CONNECTION — player-psychologist F5 + my F4 (build diversity).** Heavy builds already pay twice: ablative slag-plating is "30% slower", and "light builds see more" through the camera zoom. The only boss is "an ultra-light sniper that jumps across rooftops", the target that most rewards vision and speed. So in the first version heavy builds have a speed tax, a vision tax and a bad matchup against the climax encounter on two waves out of five. That isn't a tradeoff any more. Heavy builds are simply dominated against this content, and the "every part is a tradeoff" pillar fails for the whole heavy archetype. Camera zoom has to be costed as part of the Weight stat's downside, or the boss roster has to include something that punishes light builds.

**CONNECTION — player-psychologist F3 + feasibility-lead F3 (heat economy).** The psychologist's miss → heat → overheat → miss loop gets worse under the stat coupling feasibility-lead points out. Heat is one stat that sets both "how fast you heat up" and "how much headroom you have". Durability damage also lowers "heat tolerance". So a player who just lost a run comes back with less heat headroom, and the timing-miss heat loop then triggers sooner. My F3 death spiral and the psychologist's F3 timing spiral reinforce each other through the heat stat. I agree with the psychologist's fix (a Miss costs no extra heat). The damage gap of 200% vs 50% (4x) already makes timing matter, so the heat penalty adds punishment without adding a decision.

### Adversarial QA

**CONFLICT (partial) — adversarial-qa F3.1, the "ballast" exploit.** QA calls fitting a heavy weapon "as ballast" and letting it get shot off an exploit. I think the framing is off. You carry the weapon's full weight for the fight until the arm is disabled, and the damage taken to disable it feeds durability ("a share of the damage taken (TBD%) becomes durability damage"), which costs scrap to repair. Deliberate sacrifice therefore has a real scrap cost. The real systems issue is the one in my F4: the weight refund *softens damage regions as a punishment* and rewards glass builds that expect to lose an arm. I'd keep this MAJOR as a tuning hole and not treat it as an exploit. QA's fix (keep the disabled weapon as dead weight) is still the right one.

**CONFLICT (partial) — adversarial-qa F3.3, the soft-lock.** The finding is valid, but "the player may have no damage source left" isn't true for every build. The GDD gives two damage sources that don't use the arms. The siege mortar is a shoulder weapon, and overheating "vents damaging steam". A jump-jet build ("Each jump adds 40% heat") can overheat on purpose to do damage. That's degenerate rather than a fix, but it means the soft-lock depends on the build: arms disabled, plus a non-weapon shoulder (heatsink, flash-vent or salvage magnet), plus no reliable heat source. That also makes it more dangerous. It's rare enough to be missed in testing, and the starter loadout isn't specified, so we can't rule out that the *starter* mech is a soft-lock build. Combined with "Leaving early counts as a loss", the player has no forfeit that keeps any value. See my revision of F3 below.

**CONNECTION — adversarial-qa F5 + feasibility-lead F3 + my F4 (Power is the dominant stat).** QA works out that Overclock covers about 5 doubled fills. Push that through the stat model. Power sets "Weapon damage **and** how fast ATB bars fill", so heavy-weapon DPS scales roughly with Power × fill rate × timing multiplier. That's close to quadratic in Power, with a 4x spread from timing on top. The Cold-fusion cell gives "Power +20", plus speed, *minus* heat, and its only listed tradeoff is that it's "the most expensive part". So it has no gameplay tradeoff at all. Stack Overclock (fill ×2) on top and Power-heavy builds pull away from everything else. The pillar needs Power to carry a real cost (heat or weight per point), or damage and fill rate need to come from separate stats.

**AGREEMENT WITH A CORRECTION — adversarial-qa F1.** QA's reading of Gauntlet is the better-supported one. The garage line "In an excursion, scrap only counts once you extract … Gauntlet has no such loss" only makes sense if Gauntlet scrap *counts*, meaning it's banked. I stand by BLOCKING. QA's fix ("no bankable scrap, or a sharply reduced rate") is incomplete for the reasons given above. A reduced rate that can buy parts is still a farm for low-`p` players. Restricting what the scrap can buy is more robust than lowering the rate.

### Business Analyst

**MISREAD — business-analyst F3 on Gauntlet.** BA writes that Gauntlet "removes every penalty and 'doesn't progress your mech', so it gives casual players no reason to play", and proposes cutting it. The GDD supports the opposite reading. Run rules say "You keep your scrap", Game modes says "no scrap lost", and the garage says scrap-loss rules apply "in an excursion … Gauntlet has no such loss". As written, Gauntlet is too *rewarding*, not too empty (adversarial-qa F1, my F1). I also argue against cutting it. With a repair-only payout, Gauntlet is the only catch-up mechanism the design has. Cutting it would leave my F3 death spiral with no exit except the starter mech.

**CONFLICT — business-analyst F5 severity (MINOR) vs my F5 (MAJOR).** BA treats overlapping sinks and rarity as decoration cost. In my lane it's load-bearing. The power curve, and so the difficulty curve and runs-to-next-upgrade pacing, can't be drawn until we know which axis is vertical. Re-reading the GDD, there are actually **four** vertical axes, not three: rarity tiers, "Stat upgrade | per level", "Higher-tier weapon | per tier", and the Reactor's own tiers ("Reactor (tiered) … higher tiers give more … Higher tiers cost more scrap"). Each one stacks multiplicatively against a single fixed boss, which leads to my new F6. I agree with BA's ask to "pick one vertical progression track". I'd add that it should be tiers, with each tier scaling the part's *tradeoff* as well as its upside, so climbing tiers doesn't wear down the pillar.

**CONFLICT — business-analyst F4's freeze direction.** BA asks to freeze the prototype's 9 new parts and tune the original roughly 12. From the systems side that's the wrong half to keep. The original table writes tradeoffs as free-floating percentages ("30% slower", "torso turns 25% slower"), and those can't be expressed in the five-stat model (my F4). The Oct 22 parts are the only ones written as stat deltas ("weight -12", "+14 speed", "Power +20"). I agree with the freeze. The merge should go *toward the prototype's format*: rewrite the 12 starters as stat deltas, then cut down to 8–12 parts.

### Feasibility Lead

**CONNECTION — feasibility-lead F2 + my F4 (mortar balance).** The prototype cut the mortar's minimum range from 15 m to 4 m because "the 32-cell map is too small". Minimum range was the mortar's *only* gameplay drawback in the spec ("Can't hit within 15 m; pods explode if shot"). The prototype also adds a 3x3x3 voxel blast, and feasibility-lead F4 notes there's no shoulder damage region, so "pods explode if shot" has nothing to hit. The prototype mortar has effectively lost its tradeoff. Any balance data from the prototype will over-value it.

**CONNECTION — feasibility-lead F1 + my F3 (disconnects).** With "all play is online" and no defined disconnect rule, a dropped connection is most naturally read as "leaving early", which "counts as a loss". That gives the death spiral a third entry point the player doesn't control, after death and the QA soft-lock. Systems fix: a disconnect banks wave-3 progress (or whatever partial bank is adopted) and applies no durability damage.

**CONNECTION — feasibility-lead F4 / narrative-critic F1 / business-analyst F1 / adversarial-qa F2 + my F2 (zones as progression).** Four reviewers flag the campaign as a scope contradiction. From my lane it is also the only *difficulty and onboarding ramp* in the document (player-psychologist F2 hints at this). If it stays, its pacing has to be defined in economy terms. On a 32-cell map, level 1 (Underpass only) is roughly a third of an already small arena. The wave-3 and wave-5 boss needs "sniper perches", which "only use open zones". The Overpass income hotspot (armored transports on the highway) may sit in a sealed zone, which would gate early income. My recommendation: zone level becomes an *account-persistent* unlock earned by extracting, which counts as progression "in excursions", and it isn't lost on death. That keeps the ramp, gives the death spiral a non-scrap progress floor, and is still single-player. The two-player wording should go, per business-analyst F1.

### Narrative Critic

**CONNECTION — narrative-critic F4 + business-analyst F4 → new finding F6.** Narrative-critic sees the same sniper twice per run as a flat stakes ladder. In systems terms it's worse. Gauntlet is "the same waves", there's one map and one boss, and nothing in the GDD says enemies scale between excursions. Meanwhile player power only rises across four vertical axes (see above). The difficulty curve over a playthrough therefore *falls*. Each upgrade makes the same 5 waves easier until extraction is guaranteed and the death risk that drives the loop is gone. See F6 below.

**CONFLICT (minor) — narrative-critic F5 on automatic extraction.** I agree it needs an authored beat. I would not make extraction a *played* escape sequence (a timed run to an evac point) in the first version without numbers. That adds a sixth fail state after the wave-5 boss, which is the most loss-averse moment in the run (player-psychologist F1). A non-interactive extraction beat is the right call until the partial-bank rules exist.

### Revisions to my Round 1

- **F1 (Gauntlet farm) — stays BLOCKING, fix revised.** The preferred fix is now a **repair-only Gauntlet scrap pool**, not a discounted rate. A discount still pays struggling players more in Gauntlet than in Excursion (the `p·S − R` argument above). Restricting what the scrap buys keeps "doesn't progress your mech" true.
- **F3 (death spiral) — upgraded MAJOR → BLOCKING.** In Round 1 I treated it as a tuning risk. After the cross-read there are three paths to a total loss the player can't control and that can't be voluntarily cashed out: death, the build-dependent armless soft-lock (adversarial-qa F3) and disconnects (feasibility-lead F1). On top of that, durability lowers heat tolerance, which feeds the timing-miss spiral (player-psychologist F3). The minimum fix set is: say whether durability applies on death, a partial bank at the wave-3 kill, a forfeit option that keeps the partial bank, and repair-only Gauntlet scrap.
- **F4 (parts bypass the stat model) — stays MAJOR, scope sharpened.** I narrow it. The prototype parts already follow the model, and the 12-part table is the problem. I'm adding Power's double coupling (damage × fill rate) and the Cold-fusion cell's lack of any gameplay cost as the most concrete pillar violations.
- **F5 (sinks and rarity) — stays MAJOR, widened.** There are four vertical axes, not three: the Reactor tiers count as one. I disagree with business-analyst F5's MINOR rating for the reasons above.
- **F2 (campaign vs 5-wave structure) — stays MAJOR in my lane.** I defer to business-analyst F1 and narrative-critic F1, both BLOCKING, on the scope decision. My part is the pacing spec if zones are kept: account-persistent, unlocked by extracting, never lost on death.
- **New F6 — No enemy scaling against monotonic player power; the difficulty curve declines. MAJOR.** *Source:* "Gauntlet: the same waves"; "First version has one fixed boss"; four repeatable or tiered power sinks in Economy and Parts list; no wave-composition or scaling rules anywhere in Run rules or Enemies and AI. *Problem:* every upgrade makes the only content easier and nothing pushes back, so the risk that justifies "lose all scrap on death" goes away after some number of runs nobody has calculated. *Needs:* a per-wave composition table, an excursion-tier or threat-level rule that scales with player power or zone level, and a target run count at which a median player reaches the top of the power curve.

**Revised severity summary:** F1 BLOCKING · F2 MAJOR · F3 BLOCKING (up) · F4 MAJOR · F5 MAJOR · F6 MAJOR (new).
