# Frame-by-frame isometric sprites from AI: Scenario, the wider field, and the 3D route

Written 29 October 2026, a rerun of the puppet research (`2026-10-29-puppet-rig-workflows.md`) for the alternative kr0 asked about: "isometric instead of puppet", meaning baked frame sets in the game's high three-quarter 2:1 dimetric camera instead of a code-animated cut-out. Status: research, no decisions. Scenario facts come from the MCP catalog, skills and public workflow graphs read today (team `team_pG6MaimfJd9b9PTLYKkYR7fQ`, project `proj_T9sfQEQPZ2s4yoHgggBe1fAE`); prices are `dry_run` quotes in credits (CU) taken today with Tim-o's turnaround (`asset_fgDKMUX8fgmRfwyzQSSFqv2s`) as the input, no generation was run. Most third-party hosts (scenario.com, help.scenario.com, ludo.ai, pixellab.ai, tripo3d.ai, meshy.ai, rigbake.com, itch.io, roboticape.com, sorceress.games, dev.to, glama.ai) are outside this session's allowed network, so they were read through search snippets only; "not verified" marks those claims.

## Verdict

Three things changed since the coverage note of 28 October priced frame-by-frame at "over 1,000 credits". First, the same 12-frame grid that cost 13 credits on GPT Image 2 (`model_openai-gpt-image-2`) quotes at **4 credits on GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst** (`model_openai-gpt-image-2-5-sunburst`, 1536x1024, medium, transparent): a full 6 moves x 8 facings set is about 200 credits before re-rolls, not 1,000. Second, Scenario's own `scenario-sprite-animation` skill confirms, with numbers from live runs, exactly what we saw in `sprites/run-iso` and `jetpack-iso-r4`: a general image model drifts the feet by up to 87 px between rows and the body centre by 59 px, anchoring language and layout templates do not fix it, and the only fix is per-tile re-alignment in post. So frame-by-frame grids stay usable for one-facing screens (garage idle, cheer) and for nothing that has to turn. Third, the route most isometric games actually use, one 3D model rendered from eight cameras, is now cheap on Scenario (mesh 45, rig 20 to 40, a library animation 32 per move) and a Tripo mesh of Tim-o already exists in the project (`asset_xhPHTKBPCQgbrwErBttNdQVC`, 24 October). Its risk for Metr0niks is purely style: a rendered mesh needs a toon shader and an ink outline to pass as ligne-claire, and nobody has shown that on a pastel flat-fill look. The experiment we recommend bridges the two: render flat grey clay frames from a 3D proxy (the voxel prototype already turns Tim-o's legs and torso in every direction, so no 3D credits are needed) and let GPT Image 2.5 repaint the whole 12-cell clay grid in one 4-credit call with the hero frame as reference, which is Scenario's own `scenario-orbit-views` recipe ("the mesh fixes silhouette, scale and perspective; the picture supplies identity") applied to animation. If that holds feet and scale on a back-3/4 run, baked iso sets become viable for walk, dash and jets in all eight facings for about 4 credits per move per facing; the puppet still owns torso aim and weapon swaps, and the two can coexist (baked legs and body per facing, puppet arms on top).

## Scenario-specific findings

1. **The animation story is frame-based and video-first.** `scenario-sprite-animation` routes: pixel cycles to Retro Diffusion, "an exact grid, chosen frame count, scripted beats" to a high-adherence image model, loops to first+last-frame video, several facings to one clip per facing and cycle finished by local scripts (`cycle_prompts.py`, `cycle_frames.py`, `cycle_sheets.py`, installed with `npx skills add scenario-labs/skills --skill scenario-sprite-animation`). `scenario-consistency` says outright: "Sprite sheets and animation frames are the hard case: adjacent frames from a general image model drift in proportion, scale, and silhouette ... Frames of one character come from the video lanes." No skill or workflow mentions a 2D rig.
2. **Measured drift on the single-sheet lane** (lane-schemas reference, a GPT Image 4x4 at 2048): grounded feet at 487 / 447 / 400 px by row, body centre wandering 59 px (sd 26); "an invisible ground line" made the model draw the line at a different height per row (462, 422, 402); a layout template passed as reference was honoured for the grid and still landed rows at 486, 461, 420. Post alignment (median grounded-frame bottom per row, horizontal body-box centring on a wider canvas) brought it to 7 px / 0.3 px. Frames 13 to 16 came back as one held pose; the 16-to-1 seam measured 1.06x the largest in-cycle step. Our run-iso sheet shows the same signature: frames 1 to 4 share a pose, feet float on dust at different heights.
3. **Directional cycles, from a 5-character 40-clip production run** (directional-cycles reference): first frame at 58 percent of a 960 px square with feet at 82 percent; a flat magenta/green/blue field written into every prompt plus "no ground shadow" (the model adds one anyway, the key removes it); treadmill wording or the character walks out of frame; last frame pinned for idle and attack, free for short walks; back-view runs held only with "runs away from the viewer toward the top-right corner", naming what stays visible, pinned end, 5 s; a size warning (figure walked toward camera) means regenerate, not re-range. Seam score (seam delta over motion baseline) under 0.5 clean, over 0.8 widen the window; one heavy character went 1.08 to 0.29. Mirroring halves the work "and swaps sides", so an asymmetric character lists the mirrored facings explicitly, which for Tim-o means 8 clips per move either way.
4. **Public workflows worth copying from** (graphs read with `workflow_get`): the Run Cycle workflow's two-slot trick (character image + pose-reference sheet, "use ONLY its poses"); Pixel Lock's approval node before the expensive video step and its per-frame cleanup loop with a fixed palette to stop colour flicker; the 8-Direction generator's prompt (eight views in a circle, "only rotate around the vertical axis"). All three run on `model_openai-gpt-image-2-5-sunburst` with `background: auto`, so set `transparent` on a copy.
5. **Prices today** (dry runs): GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst 12-frame sheet 4 CU (medium, 1536x1024, transparent, 1 reference), 14 CU at 2048x1536 high; GPT Image 2 same payload 13 CU; Nano Banana 2.1 (`model_google-gemini-nano-banana-2-1`, 14 references, no transparent output) 9 CU; Veo 3.1 Lite 4 s 24 CU; Wan 2.2 480p 30 CU; Luma Ray 3.2 loop 540p 30 CU; Kling V3 Standard 3 s 51 CU, Pro 68; Seedance 2.5 draft 84, 720p 186; FLUX.3 first-last 170; Video to Image Sequence 2; Video Chroma Key 2; Pixelcut 2; BiRefNet video 4; Video to Layers 76; Image Slicer 1; Pixelate 5 per image; Retro Diffusion Animation 15; Tripo 3.1 single or multiview 45; Meshy Rigging 20; Uthana Rigging 40; Meshy Animation 32; Uthana Text to Motion 208; Claude Opus 5.5 3D 400; Cartwheel rigging 1,440, text-to-motion 1,536. Concurrency: three jobs at a time (HTTP 429 at twelve).
6. **The 3D chain is complete except the render.** `scenario-3d` describes concept -> image-to-3D (multiview, `texture: false` or `delight: false` for painted shading) -> UV/retopo -> rig last on the shipping topology; humanoid riggers take a front-facing T/A-pose mesh; `model_meshy-animation` retargets a library clip by `actionId` and can `extract_armature`; `scenario-video-to-motion` covers `moveInPlace`, `loop`, axis fixes and warns the rig is the member's own. Rendering eight cameras is Blender work: `scenario-orbit-views` ships `render_grounded.py` (16-camera ring, 22.5 degrees apart, elevation flag, GLB in) and `scenario-blender` drives an open Blender scene through a local MCP server, but neither produces sprites by itself. The existing project meshes (`asset_xhPHTKBPCQgbrwErBttNdQVC` and `asset_qRoCff2ZdqxPzyvFu1Ke4tNf`, Tripo P2 from 24 Oct; `asset_7PHvfnsVQ2rLUm52DfpndTNf`, Tripo 3.1) were not inspected for pose or quality here.

## Beyond Scenario: how people get consistent frames in 2025 to 2026

- **(a) Direct image models drawing a grid.** Google's own prompting guide suggests "3x3 grid, sequence, frame by frame" sheets and cutting cells out ([dev.to/googleai](https://dev.to/googleai/nano-banana-pro-prompting-guide-strategies-1h9n)). The most useful write-up ([roboticape, March 2026](https://roboticape.com/2026/03/07/generating-game-sprites-with-gemini-image-generation-nano-banana-pro-lessons-learned/), snippet) moved to a flat #00FF00 field with a white outline around each sprite, found the model never spaces cells uniformly even when asked, and sliced by connected components. Walk-cycle guides report boots sliding and head size changing between cells that look fine alone ([geekvibesnation](https://geekvibesnation.com/nano-banana-pro-sprite-sheets-make-the-walk-cycle-work)). Model comparisons in 2026 rate Nano Banana 2 and GPT Image 2 highest for identity, Seedream 5 "good", Flux 2 strongest on aesthetics ([atlascloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/best-ai-image-editing-models-2026)), none on frames. Consistency tricks that are actually evidenced: a pose-reference sheet in a second slot (Scenario's run-cycle workflow), the approved still as reference in every call, byte-identical baseline prompt, transparent output or a key colour far from the palette, and alignment in post. Multiple facings: one call per facing; a mirror flips the weapons. Style fidelity: best of all routes, it is drawn in the style. Cost: 4 to 14 CU per 12-frame sheet; turnaround minutes.
- **(b) Image-to-video, then extract.** The pattern everyone converges on: prompt a fixed camera and a plain or green field, 3 to 5 s clip, ffmpeg or Scenario's extractor at 8 to 12 fps, chroma key with tolerance for anti-aliased edges, trim to a loop window, pack ([TamerinTECH skill](https://github.com/TamerinTECH/claude-skill-klingai-animation), [mikeveerman Claude After Dark](https://mikeveerman.be/blog/github-2026-01-17-claude-after-dark/), [loopsprite extractor](https://loopsprite.com/extract/), [MP4toSprite](https://yewlark.itch.io/mp4tosprite)). Video models produce "muted or textured greens, not pure #00FF00", so thresholds alone fail; Veo is praised for first+last frame control, Kling for spatial consistency from the input, Wan 2.7 is open weights (Apache 2.0, 24 GB GPU) if we ever self-host. Third-party model shoot-outs on character stability rank Seedance 2.x, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1 close with undocumented methods ([fuser](https://fuser.studio/articles/seedance-vs-kling-vs-veo), [aijourn](https://aijourn.com/seedance-2-0-vs-kling-3-0-vs-veo-3-1-ai-video-benchmark-test-for-2026/)); none tests feet or loops. One itch developer called the Gemini + Veo 3 + extract + key route "slow and labor-intensive". Facings: one clip per facing, back views unreliable (Scenario's own numbers above). Style: re-rendered by the video model; flat ink can pick up shading and motion blur. Cost: 24 to 190 CU per clip plus 4 to 6 CU cleanup; 10 to 15 minutes per clip with review.
- **(c) Image-to-3D, rig, render.** Vendor docs: Tripo rigging 25 credits per model plus 10 per retargeted clip from 100+ presets, text-to-motion 1 to 10 s, Pro plan about $20 per month for 3,000 credits ([developers.tripo3d.ai](https://developers.tripo3d.ai/en/pricing), [ludo auto-rigging roundup](https://ludo.ai/compare/best-ai-auto-rigging-tools)); Meshy image-to-3D 20 credits, auto-rig 5, animation 3 per call, library of 500+ clips ([docs.meshy.ai](https://docs.meshy.ai/en/webapp/pricing)); Rodin Gen-2.5 and Hunyuan3D produce the mesh and leave rigging to others; Mixamo is still the free humanoid rig + clip source and has a Blender add-on ([meshy Mixamo guide](https://www.meshy.ai/tutorials/how-to-use-mixamo-with-meshy)). The usual chain is image-to-3D -> Mixamo or vendor rig -> Blender -> 8-direction script -> sheet, and it is what the harumaxy author retreated to after diffusion failed on aim and jump. Facings: all eight from one render pass, mirroring unnecessary. Style: PBR renders look like 3D; a toon shader with 2 to 3 bands plus Freestyle lines gets to cel, matching sun-bleached pastel ink is unproven. For Tim-o specifically, a rig only needs hips, two knees, two shoulders, two elbows and a torso yaw, and the pile-bunker and dash moves are not in any library, so hand-keying in Blender or Uthana text-to-motion (208 CU per clip) is the realistic path. Cost on Scenario: about 45 + 20 to 40 + 32 per library move; hours of Blender per character for shader, camera and render setup.
- **(d) Sprite-specific tools.** PixelLab is the only one with a real skeleton animator and 8 rotations from your own image, at 16 to 128 px pixel art ([pixellab docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/skeleton-animation)); Retro Diffusion is the pixel route inside Scenario; Ludo's "rotation generator" and sprite presets are 2D AI, not a 3D render ([ludo rotation](https://ludo.ai/tools/sprite-rotation-generator)); Layer.ai wraps Gemini 3.1 Flash Image grids. None targets a 300 px flat-ink character, and none can be fed from our one-source Scenario pipeline.

## Verification: how to check a frame set

- **Local measurements per frame** (what the Scenario skill and the `rig-parts` gate both do): alpha bounding box height (character height spread; flag over 3 percent), feet line = lowest opaque row of the body blob in grounded frames (flag spread over 1 percent of height; airborne frames borrow neighbours' baseline), horizontal centroid of the widest column run (flag over 1 percent), silhouette IoU against the previous frame and against the clay or reference pose (a sudden drop is a redrawn part), per-part colour histogram against the hero frame (a pack or gun that changes hue is drift, per `scenario-consistency`: "a named part gained, lost, recoloured or reshaped is drift; a part that moves with the action is animation"). Write these as a small script in `tools/` and run it on every sliced set before the review page; our review page already has "same size" and "anchor feet" toggles, so the numbers can sit beside them.
- **Loop seam**: mean absolute pixel delta between consecutive frames (motion baseline) versus the delta across the seam; seam at or under the window's own baseline closes, over 0.8x on walk/run means the window is wrong before the frames are. Exclude frame 0 of a video clip, which often diverges from every later frame.
- **Onion skin and overlay**: the review page's frame player; in Aseprite, onion skin plus the community MCP tools `check_animation` (per-frame bounds, ground row, centroid, colour drift, loop vs one-shot) and `diff_sprites` (silhouette vs interior change counts) ([aseprite-mcp-studio](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/mhpm/aseprite-mcp-studio/tools/check_animation), [aseprite-mcp](https://github.com/MalloyTheDev/aseprite-mcp)); [sprita's frame-consistency guide](https://sprita.app/learn/frame-consistency) frames jitter as root, scale, timing or transparency changes and says to fix it at the earliest stage it appears.
- **Grid hygiene before slicing** (lane-schemas): count the cells, read the cell edges for clipped effects, count distinct poses (a beat range that names one end state comes back as one held pose), check one tile's alpha before slicing the rest, and verify extracted-frame order from the job's `assetIds` with a contact sheet, never from timestamps.
- **Re-placement by feature matching** (from yesterday's note, `spine-animation-ai`): SIFT + RANSAC similarity of each part back onto the hero frame measures per-part scale and rotation drift; on a frame set this gives a pack-drift number per frame.
