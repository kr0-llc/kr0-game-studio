# From AI art to a rigged cut-out puppet: workflows, tools, and what Scenario offers

Written 29 October 2026 for the Tim-o puppet pipeline (`art/metr0niks/characters/tim-o/puppet/generation-pipeline.md`). Status: research, no decisions. Sources are cited inline; "not verified" marks claims that come from vendor pages or search snippets only. Several hosts (scenario.com, help.scenario.com, feedback.scenario.com, arxiv.org, medium.com, riggyapp.com, anysplit.net, godmodeai.co, phaser.io, layer.ai) are outside this session's allowed network, so their pages were read through search snippets, the Scenario MCP catalog and skills, and GitHub, not fetched directly.

## Verdict

Our approach (draw the character once as a hero frame, then edit it into an exploded sheet with ball caps and sockets, then slice and seed pivots from the caps) is the same shape as every credible 2025–2026 workflow we found: Meta's Animated Drawings, the See-through and Bunraku papers, Riggy and SpineFactory, the open-source `rig-parts` and `spine-animation-ai` projects, and Scenario's own "Image to Layers" and Seedream "Layerize" tools all start from ONE assembled drawing and take it apart; none generate parts one by one, and the only Scenario user asking for parts sheets ("Generate character part sheets for 2d bone rigging") expects a multistep process, not a one-shot. Where the field differs from us is the separation step: the mature tools segment the assembled image and inpaint what was hidden (layer decomposition), while we ask the image model to redraw the figure exploded. For a hard-surface mech with ball joints drawn into the art, our exploded-sheet edit is defensible and cheap (4 credits), but two upgrades are worth piloting: (1) run Scenario's `Image to Layers` or `Seedream 5.0 Flash Layerize` on the hero frame as a second, segmentation-based route and compare; (2) add the reassembly check that `rig-parts` does (recomposite the parts at their seeded pivots over the hero frame and measure uncovered and wrong-colour pixels) as an automatic gate in `timo_exploded.py` before the harness runs. Gemini (Nano Banana) is not clearly better than GPT Image at keeping identity across an edit; the evidence is mixed and anecdotal, so test both on the same hero frame rather than switch.

## Scenario-specific findings

1. **Segmentation and layers exist as first-party tools, not as a character workflow.** The catalog (read through the Scenario MCP on 29 October 2026, project `proj_T9sfQEQPZ2s4yoHgggBe1fAE`) lists `Image to Layers` (`model_scenario-image-layers-extractor`: `image`, `separationInstruction`, `maxLayers` 1–10, `inpaintModel` flux2-klein-9b / flux2-dev / gpt-image-2, `inpaintPrompt`), `Seedream 5.0 Flash Layerize` (`model_bytedance-seedream-5-0-flash-layerize`: `image`, optional `prompt` with `<bbox>x1 y1 x2 y2</bbox>` targets normalised 0–1000, `size` auto/1K/1.5K/2K, up to 16 layers), `SAM 3.1 Image` (`model_meta-sam-3-1-image`: text/box/point prompts, `applyMask` for an alpha cut-out), `Scenario Detection` (`model_scenario-detection`: `pose`, `segmentation`, `canny`, `depth`...), and `Video to Layers`. The `scenario-image-editing` skill routes "cutouts, relight, layers, vector" through `recommend` with `capability="img2img"` and warns that layer extraction returns one asset per piece.
2. **No Spine / skeletal / puppet export.** Nothing in the catalog, skills (`scenario-sprite-animation`, `scenario-side-view-game-kit`, `scenario-consistency`, `scenario-image-editing`, `scenario-gemini-image`) or help pages mentions Spine, DragonBones, Live2D or cut-out rigs. Scenario's animation story is frame-based: sprite sheets, image-to-video cycles, the Image Slicer, Grid Maker and frame extractor. The `scenario-consistency` skill says plainly that adjacent frames from a general image model "drift in proportion, scale, and silhouette" and routes animation to video lanes.
3. **The parts-sheet app and the user request.** [AI 2D Animation Rigging Sheet Generator](https://www.scenario.com/apps/2d-animation-rigging-sheet) is marketed to Spine/Live2D/Moho users as a one-shot parts sheet from a concept (model and prompt not readable here). The feedback board post [Generate character part sheets for 2d bone rigging](https://feedback.scenario.com/p/generate-character-part-sheets-for-2d-bone-rigging) asks for "an exploded parts view" from one character image and says one-shot AI is "doubtful", suggesting a multistep process; no staff reply was visible in the snippet.
4. **Documented workflows that come closest.** [Animating a 2D character using Scenario and Blender](https://help.scenario.com/animating-a-2d-character-using-scenario-and-blender) and [Creating a 2D platformer animated character using Scenario and Blender](https://help.scenario.com/creating-a-2d-platformer-animated-character-using-scenario-and-blender): prompt "neutral pose", remove the background, import into Blender, add an armature, pose it. Separation into parts is done by hand in an image editor. [Generate Character Turnarounds](https://www.scenario.com/blog/generate-character-turnarounds-scenario) and its help article: "Edit with Prompt" using Gemini 2.5 (Nano Banana), Flux Kontext, GPT, Seedream Edit or Runway Gen4 from one reference; newer Nano Banana Pro, Seedream 4.5 and the "True 4 Pose" app "often achieve similar or better results with fewer steps"; fix drift by masking and inpainting in Retouch. The [Character Turnaround Kontext](https://www.scenario.com/models/character-turnaround-kontext) LoRA gives 4 views on white.
5. **Models users and Scenario recommend for identity-preserving edits**: Gemini 3.1 Flash / 3.0 Pro / 3.1 Lite (`model_google-gemini-3-1-flash`, `model_google-gemini-pro-image-editing`, `model_google-gemini-3-1-flash-lite-image`; up to 14 reference images, no mask or seed), GPT Image 2.5 (our current), Seedream 5.0 Flash (10 references), Flux 2 Pro/Turbo Edit, Qwen Edit 2511, Ideogram 4.5 Precise Edit (mask + "leave the rest untouched"), Krea 3 Edit ("everything you did not mention stays untouched"), Kling O3 I2I. The `scenario-gemini-image` skill's rule for edits: state the change and what must survive it, assign reference roles by position.
6. **Consistency guidance from the skills** that applies to the exploded-sheet edit: baseline-plus-delta prompt kept byte-identical between runs; the hero frame in the reference slot (as an array); never chain output to output; `asset_detect`/Detection `canny` or `depth` as a control map when the layout must not move (models with `controlnet` capability only).

## Gemini vs GPT Image for the exploded-sheet edit

- No controlled benchmark of identity preservation under edits was found. Third-party comparisons disagree: a nine-view avatar test found Nano Banana Pro more stable on face geometry than GPT Image 2 ([virse.ai](https://www.virse.ai/blog/chatgpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana)); another names GPT Image 2 the winner for single-character consistency and "conversational revisions that keep an approved composition" ([tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-pro/), [aigf.love](https://aigf.love/blog/gpt-image-2-vs-nano-banana-ai-characters/)); img.ly's benchmark scores Gemini 2.5 Flash Image 3.5/5 on consistency ([img.ly](https://img.ly/ai-benchmarks/models/gemini-25-flash-image/)). Google's own blog admits character consistency "may not always" hold ([blog.google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/image-generation-prompting-tips/)). Scenario lists both as peers for turnarounds (finding 4).
- Practical differences that matter more than the identity score: Gemini has no transparent output (a sprite write-up had to key a flat green background and keep a white outline buffer, [roboticape.com](https://roboticape.com/2026/03/07/generating-game-sprites-with-gemini-image-generation-nano-banana-pro-lessons-learned/)), no mask, no seed; GPT Image 2.5 gives us transparent PNGs today. Gemini takes up to 14 references, so the hero frame, the turnaround side view and the previous exploded sheet can all be passed with roles.
- Exploded-view prompts exist for Nano Banana Pro ([Medium prompt collection](https://medium.com/@GrsAi.com/nano-banana-pro-exploded-view-and-character-breakdown-engineering-prompt-collection-with-1-4k-api-c4c855444eb0), mecha "design sheet with exploded views" at [nanoprompts.org](https://nanoprompts.org/lab/2025/game-character-sheet-design)) but they target product/diagram looks (parchment backgrounds, labels), not rig-ready parts; none was tested. Recommendation: one A/B run (about 4 credits each) of the same exploded-sheet prompt on `model_google-gemini-3-1-flash` with the hero frame as image 1, scored by the reassembly check below, before changing the pipeline.

## Verification: do the parts line up?

- **Recomposite error map** (`rig-parts`): assemble the parts at their rig positions, diff against the painting, write an image that is red where no part covers a painted pixel and blue where a part has the wrong colour; a `SETUP_POSE_VS_SOURCE` check and a seam metric (`mean |d| <= 1.0` at the setup pose) gate the build, and "uncovered hole" lines are listed per part. This is the standard we should adopt.
- **Re-placement by feature matching** (`spine-animation-ai`): SIFT + RANSAC similarity transform from each cut part back onto the original image (≥4 inliers, template-match fallback for small parts) recovers where each part sat and at what scale; overlapping pairs are ordered by which part's pixels match the original, giving draw order. For our exploded sheet this directly measures "did the model keep the drawing, size and angle" per piece and yields the rest offsets without hand typing.
- **Onion-skin / overlay in tools**: Toon Boom Harmony and Live2D document onion skin for poses ([docs.toonboom.com](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-21/premium/cut-out-animation/use-onion-skin-cut-out-animation.html), [docs.live2d.com](https://docs.live2d.com/en/cubism-editor-manual/onion-skin/)); Spine has no reference-overlay feature, users pre-align in Photoshop and set stacked images to 0,0 ([esotericsoftware forum](https://esotericsoftware.com/forum/d/4837-align-tool)). Our Puppet Rigging Lab already renders live; adding a half-transparent hero frame under the rest pose is the manual version of the check.
- Our harness measures cap exposure and socket gap per joint; it does not yet compare the assembled rest pose to the hero frame. Both checks are complementary: the recomposite catches a redrawn or rescaled part, the harness catches a bad pivot.

## Recommended workflow (reference image → rigged parts)

1. **Hero frame** (unchanged): one assembled render from the game camera, rest pose with every joint visible as a ball, transparent background, turnaround as reference. kr0 approves it against the turnaround (on-model gate). ~4 credits.
2. **Separate, two routes, same input.** (a) Exploded-sheet edit as now (GPT Image 2.5; one Gemini 3.1 Flash A/B run). (b) New: `Image to Layers` or `Seedream Layerize` on the hero frame with a part list ("torso, back pack, near upper arm, near forearm with minigun, far upper arm, far forearm with pile bunker, near thigh, near shin, far thigh, far shin"), bbox targets taken from the hero frame; `dry_run` both, expect one asset per layer. Route (b) keeps every pixel where it was, so no re-placement is needed; its risk is what the hole-fill paints at the shoulders and knees.
3. **Re-place and re-check (new, automatic).** In `timo_exploded.py`: for route (a) match each sliced piece back onto the hero frame (SIFT/RANSAC similarity, as in `spine-animation-ai`) to get its original position, scale and the draw order from overlap colour; reject a piece whose scale differs from 1.0 by more than a few percent or that finds no match (it was redrawn). For route (b) positions are known. Then recomposite all pieces at those positions over the hero frame and write `parts/<ver>-recomposite-error.png` (red uncovered, blue wrong colour) plus a per-part coverage number; fail the run above a threshold kr0 sets (start at 2% uncovered per part).
4. **Seed pivots** from the sand caps and sockets (unchanged); for a cap hidden under a knee plate on route (b), copy visible pixels then inpaint (Textoon's trick) or keep route (a)'s explicit caps. Pivots live in data; a hand-typed pivot is a note for the next sheet edit.
5. **Harness** (`tools/rig-harness`): rest, walk, aim, stress; cap exposure < 0.05, gap 0, plus the new rest-pose-vs-hero-frame overlay score from step 3.
6. **Review** in the Puppet Rigging Lab with the hero frame as a toggleable underlay; kr0 marks Keep/Fix/Redo. A Redo is an edit of the sheet (route a) or a re-run of the layer tool with a tighter bbox/instruction (route b), hero frame as image 1, "keep every other piece exactly as it is".
7. **Enemies** go through the same steps; the loader's four legs and the boss's underside sockets are where route (b)'s bbox targeting may beat the exploded-sheet prompt.
